# What may be said, and what needs a lawyer

The AIOS checks every page, ad, email and script against this list before Chris sees it. This list is a guardrail, not legal advice: **a lawyer who knows this industry reviews the model, the site language and the policies before the first client launches.**

## Never, in anything a buyer, client or end customer sees

- Any health or medical claim: treats, cures, heals, prevents, recovery, weight loss, anti-aging, "boosts" anything about a body.
- Dosing, protocols, injection or administration guidance, or "how I use it".
- Testimonials, before-and-afters or influencer posts about effects on a person.
- "FDA approved", "pharmaceutical grade" or "safe", unless the lawyer approves that exact phrase for that exact product.
- Income promises to buyers of the build-out ("making money in the first month") without documented, typical results Chris approves.
- Prices, results or reviews the AIOS made up.

## Always

- The product-use language the lawyer approves , the same on every page.
- Certificates of analysis on file for every product listed.
- Clear terms, privacy, shipping and refund policies before launch.
- Age and eligibility checks the lawyer requires.

## Questions for the lawyer, before launch

1. Is the model as sold (site, stock, processor, launch) allowed in each market the buyers sell into, and what must each buyer do themselves?
2. Exactly what product-use and disclaimer language goes on the site, ads and emails?
3. Which products, if any, must never be listed?
4. What the build-out's own sales pages may say about buyers' income.
5. What the payment processors and ad platforms require, and which ones allow this category at all.

## Where it gets checked

Before any draft reaches Chris: the Peptide Operator reads it against this file, and lists any line it changed and why. A draft that needs a claim checked by the lawyer says so on its first line.
